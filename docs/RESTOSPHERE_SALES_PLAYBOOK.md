# RestoSphere Sales Playbook

## Positioning

RestoSphere is a restaurant operations platform for independent restaurants, cafes, QSRs, hotel restaurants, and growing outlet groups that want one controlled workflow from POS Order to KOT/KDS, billing, payment reconciliation, inventory, staff permissions, QR ordering, CRM, loyalty, and reports.

Its practical difference is a direct onboarding model with a broad operational workflow and scoped multi-outlet controls, supported by ANOVA implementation rather than a software-only handoff.

## Ideal Customer Profiles

### ICP A: Single-Outlet Restaurant

- Team: 5–25 staff
- Pain: manual billing, table confusion, KOT mistakes, weak reporting
- Useful modules: POS, tables, KOT/KDS, billing, cash/payment reconciliation, QR, staff
- Buying trigger: new outlet, billing replacement, operational errors
- Objection: “We already manage this manually.”
- Angle: start with one workflow and one trained team

### ICP B: Cafe / QSR

- Team: 5–30 staff
- Pain: speed, takeaway volume, staff permissions, menu availability
- Useful modules: POS, menu, staff, billing, QR, reports, loyalty
- Buying trigger: rising order volume or multiple shifts
- Objection: “Implementation will slow us down.”
- Angle: focused menu/table/staff setup with a short pilot

### ICP C: Small Hotel Restaurant

- Team: 10–50 staff
- Pain: outlet coordination, billing, room/restaurant operational visibility, inventory
- Useful modules: POS, tables, billing, KOT/KDS, inventory, CRM, reports, subscriptions
- Buying trigger: tighter control across restaurant operations
- Objection: “We need flexibility for hotel workflows.”
- Angle: map the pilot to one restaurant outlet first

### ICP D: Multi-Outlet Group

- Team: 30–200 staff
- Pain: outlet isolation, consolidated oversight, staff access, reporting
- Useful modules: multi-outlet, POS, KDS, inventory, reports/BI, staff, central kitchen where applicable
- Buying trigger: new outlet or inconsistent operating practices
- Objection: “Can you prove scale?”
- Angle: controlled pilot, transparent capacity disclosure, measured expansion

## Value Propositions

### Faster POS and Billing

CLIENT PROBLEM: Manual entry and disconnected billing slow service.  
RESTOSPHERE SOLUTION: POS Orders, tables, billing, receipts, and payment status share one workflow.  
BUSINESS BENEFIT: Staff work from one operational record.

### Order to KOT/KDS

CLIENT PROBLEM: Kitchen misses or duplicates handwritten tickets.  
RESTOSPHERE SOLUTION: An accepted Order creates the authoritative KOT/KDS flow.  
BUSINESS BENEFIT: Front-of-house and kitchen share order status.

### Controlled Multi-Outlet Operations

CLIENT PROBLEM: Staff see or change the wrong outlet’s operational data.  
RESTOSPHERE SOLUTION: Restaurant and outlet scope is enforced server-side.  
BUSINESS BENEFIT: Clearer ownership and safer operations.

### Payment and Reconciliation Visibility

CLIENT PROBLEM: Cash, digital, split, and partial payments are difficult to reconcile.  
RESTOSPHERE SOLUTION: Payment records, idempotency, reconciliation, and audit workflows.  
BUSINESS BENEFIT: Fewer unexplained payment states.

### Inventory and Recipes

CLIENT PROBLEM: Stock movements and recipe consumption are hard to trace.  
RESTOSPHERE SOLUTION: Inventory, recipes, units, movements, wastage, and procurement workflows.  
BUSINESS BENEFIT: Better operational visibility without treating client-provided opening stock as automatic truth.

### QR, CRM, Loyalty, and Reports

CLIENT PROBLEM: Customer engagement and business review are fragmented.  
RESTOSPHERE SOLUTION: QR ordering, CRM, loyalty, dashboards, reports, and BI.  
BUSINESS BENEFIT: More usable information around service and customers.

## Claims Policy

BDEs may claim verified modules only. Do not claim:

- Enterprise-scale capacity proof
- Full offline transaction/payment/KOT authority
- Live Zomato/Swiggy integration unless separately connected and validated
- Guaranteed uptime or settlement timing
- Unlimited customization
- Tax or legal advice

## Lead Qualification

HOT: Clear operational pain, decision maker engaged, budget/timeline discussed, demo requested.  
WARM: Pain exists and interest is real, but decision maker/timing is unresolved.  
COLD: No urgent pain, no timeline, or no meaningful engagement.

## Discovery Questionnaire

- How many outlets do you operate today?
- How many staff use the restaurant workflow?
- What is the normal daily Order volume?
- What is the dine-in/takeaway/delivery mix?
- Which billing/POS process do you use now?
- Where do Order, KOT, table, payment, or inventory mistakes occur?
- How are cash and digital payments reconciled?
- Do you need QR ordering?
- Do you need inventory/recipes/procurement?
- Do you need CRM or loyalty?
- What reports does the owner/manager review?
- Who is the decision maker?
- What is the target go-live date?
- What must be true after a successful trial?

## Outreach

Use the short templates in [RESTOSPHERE_WHATSAPP_PACK.md](RESTOSPHERE_WHATSAPP_PACK.md). Keep outreach personalized and permission-based. Do not spam or promise features outside the verified product.

Phone opening:

“Hello, I’m [NAME] from ANOVA Technologies. We help restaurants connect POS, kitchen tickets, billing, and operational visibility. I’m calling to understand how your team handles Orders and KOT today. If there is a real gap, we can schedule a short demo; is now a suitable time for one question?”

LinkedIn connection:

“I work with ANOVA on restaurant operations software: POS, KOT/KDS, billing, staff access, and reporting. We are speaking with independent restaurants about practical pilots. Happy to connect.”

Email subject: `A practical POS + KOT pilot for [RESTAURANT]`

Email CTA: invite the owner/operations decision maker to a 15-minute demo focused on their current bottleneck.

Walk-in flow: introduce ANOVA, ask what the restaurant uses for billing/KOT, identify one pain, show a two-minute safe demo, and book a full demo. Do not attempt a full sale at the counter.

## Sales Pipeline

Lead → Contacted → Qualified → Demo Booked → Demo Completed → Trial Started → Trial Active → Commercial Discussion → Won/Lost.

## CRM Fields

Business, owner/contact, phone, city, outlet count, current POS, pain, lead source, stage, next follow-up, demo date, trial start/end, quoted plan, status, notes, implementation owner.

## Quotation Template

- Client and restaurant
- Selected plan
- Number of outlets
- Monthly subscription
- Annual subscription if selected
- Setup/onboarding fee
- Custom work quote if any
- Applicable taxes
- Validity date
- Payment terms placeholder
- Notes and known limitations

## Referrals and Testimonials

Ask a satisfied pilot client for a truthful testimonial only after permission. Obtain written permission before naming the restaurant or using its logo. Referral incentives require approved commercial terms and must not create an unsustainable promise.

## Lost Deals

Record the reason without arguing: price, features, timing, competition, trust, or internal decision. Set a future follow-up only with permission and preserve the reason for product/sales learning.

## First 10 Customers

Prioritize Palanpur and nearby Gujarat cities, independent restaurants, cafes/QSRs, and small hotel restaurants with accessible owners and a willingness to run a measured pilot. Avoid large chains until provider operations and capacity evidence are stronger.

## Daily Activity Guidance

Track activity rather than promise conversions:

- 10–20 targeted calls
- 10–20 personalized WhatsApp/email touches
- 2–5 walk-in conversations
- 2–3 qualified demos
- Same-day follow-up for active trials
