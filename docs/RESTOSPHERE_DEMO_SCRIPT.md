# RestoSphere Demo Script

Use a dedicated demo tenant or approved safe demo data. Never use another restaurant’s customer data.

## Five-Minute Demo

### 1. Login and Dashboard: 45 seconds

WHAT TO CLICK: Login, select outlet, open dashboard.  
WHAT TO SAY: “This is the operational view for the restaurant team.”  
VALUE: Start with today’s work, not a feature tour.  
DO NOT OVER-EXPLAIN: Infrastructure or database details.

### 2. Table and POS Order: 90 seconds

WHAT TO CLICK: Open a table, add two menu items, add a note, submit.  
WHAT TO SAY: “The team creates one authoritative restaurant Order.”  
VALUE: Faster entry and fewer duplicate/manual steps.

### 3. KOT/KDS: 60 seconds

WHAT TO CLICK: Open KDS and show the new KOT.  
WHAT TO SAY: “The kitchen receives the same Order context.”  
VALUE: Front-of-house and kitchen stay aligned.

### 4. Billing: 60 seconds

WHAT TO CLICK: Open the bill/receipt flow and show payment status.  
WHAT TO SAY: “Billing and payment state remain connected to the Order.”  
VALUE: Easier service and reconciliation.

### 5. Trial: 45 seconds

WHAT TO CLICK: Show the trial/onboarding next step.  
WHAT TO SAY: “We configure one controlled pilot, train the team, and review actual usage.”  
VALUE: The client can evaluate fit with support.

## Fifteen-to-Twenty-Minute Demo

1. Login/outlet/dashboard: establish the business context.
2. Tables: show availability and active order context.
3. POS: create dine-in/takeaway examples, notes, menu availability.
4. KOT/KDS: show the lifecycle and outlet scope.
5. Billing: receipt, cash, payment state, partial/split capability where relevant.
6. QR: show a safe table QR/menu flow and explain the same authoritative Order/KOT path.
7. Menu: categories, prices, availability.
8. Inventory/recipes: show stock/movement/recipe workflow only if relevant.
9. CRM/loyalty: show customer and engagement value.
10. Staff/RBAC: show role/outlet access.
11. Reports/BI: show operational review.
12. Trial/onboarding: agree scope, users, data, and next meeting.

At every step ask: “Would this remove a problem your team has today?”

## Thirty-Minute Demo

Use the 15–20 minute sequence, then add:

- Multi-outlet context and outlet switching.
- Inventory/procurement workflow if the client uses stock control.
- Central-kitchen workflow only where it is genuinely relevant.
- CRM and loyalty examples.
- Reports/date/outlet review.
- Support/runbook expectations.
- Pilot success criteria and handover.

## Demo Failure Plan

If the live demo network fails:

1. Say so plainly.
2. Do not fake a live result.
3. Use approved screenshots/video only if available.
4. Reschedule the live workflow.
5. Record the prospect’s questions for the next demonstration.
